\section*{Sources}

This note compares the per-hex objective of HexOpt in two sources.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]\sloppy
  \item \textbf{Paper.} H.~Tong and Y.~J.~Zhang, ``Fast and Robust Hexahedral Mesh Optimization via Augmented Lagrangian, L-BFGS, and Line Search,'' \href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.11656}{arXiv:2410.11656}, Equations~(2.5) and (2.6).
  \item \textbf{conspire.} Branch \texttt{msj} at commit \texttt{515c78cee}, draft \href{https://github.com/mrbuche/conspire.rs/pull/251}{mrbuche/conspire.rs\#251}, file \texttt{src/\allowbreak geometry/\allowbreak mesh/\allowbreak quality/\allowbreak improve/\allowbreak msj/\allowbreak mod.rs}, function \texttt{terms}.
\end{itemize}

$h$ is one hex element of the mesh $H$, and $\eb$ is its average edge length.
$\SJ(h)$ is the minimum of the scaled Jacobian over nine points of $h$: its eight corners and its body center (paper, Section~2.1).
$J(h)$ is the minimum of the Jacobian over the same nine points.
conspire computes both minimums in \texttt{terms}, each over all nine points.
$\Theta$ is the threshold.
Both sources hold $\eb$ fixed when they take the gradient.

\section*{Definitions}

The paper defines
\begin{equation*}
  \ReHQJ_{\text{paper}}(h, \Theta) =
  \begin{cases}
    J(h) / \eb, & J(h) \le 0, \\
    \SJ(h) \, \eb^2, & J(h) > 0,\ \SJ(h) \le \Theta, \\
    \Theta, & \SJ(h) > \Theta.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation*}
conspire implements
\begin{equation*}
  \ReHQJ_{\text{conspire}}(h, \Theta) =
  \begin{cases}
    J(h) / \eb, & J(h) \le 0, \\
    \SJ(h) \, \eb^2, & J(h) > 0,\ \SJ(h) \le \Theta, \\
    \Theta \, \eb^2, & \SJ(h) > \Theta.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation*}
The two differ only in the third case.

\section*{Units}

$J = \det(e_0, e_1, e_2)$, where each edge vector has units of length.
$\SJ$ and $\Theta$ have no units.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
  \toprule
  case & paper & conspire \\
  \midrule
  $J \le 0$ & length$^2$ & length$^2$ \\
  $J > 0$, $\SJ \le \Theta$ & length$^2$ & length$^2$ \\
  $\SJ > \Theta$ & \textbf{none} & length$^2$ \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\section*{Behavior at the threshold}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Continuity.}
    At $\SJ = \Theta$, the second case gives $\Theta \, \eb^2$.
    The conspire form gives the same value, so the objective is continuous at $\Theta$.
    The paper's form gives $\Theta$, so the objective jumps by $\Theta (\eb^2 - 1)$ unless $\eb = 1$.
    Neither form is differentiable at $\Theta$.
    The slope drops to zero there in both, by design.

  \item \textbf{The sign of the jump.}
    The minimized function is
    \begin{equation*}
      L = -\sum_{h} \ReHQJ(h, \Theta) + \cdots,
    \end{equation*}
    as in Equation~(2.8).
    Under the paper's form, a hex that rises above $\Theta$ changes $L$ by $+\Theta (\eb^2 - 1)$.
    For $\eb > 1$, $L$ rises.
    The Armijo condition requires $L$ to fall, so the jump counts against every step that lifts such a hex above $\Theta$.
    For $\eb < 1$, $L$ falls, so the jump rewards the crossing instead.
    The sign depends on the length unit of the mesh.
    \begin{itemize}
      \item On the snapped bone octree in the reference frame, $\eb$ runs from 0.644 to 2.81, with a median of 1.253.
        The same mesh has hexes on both sides of $\eb = 1$.
      \item On the scale-5 bone mesh in the STL's own units, $\eb$ runs from 0.009 to 0.034.
    \end{itemize}
    Under the conspire form, the crossing changes $L$ by zero.

  \item \textbf{Units.}
    Under the conspire form, all three cases have units of length$^2$.
    conspire's types enforce this: each term is a \texttt{Quantity<Area>}.
\end{enumerate}

For a hex that stays above $\Theta$ through a step, both forms are constants.
They give the same gradient and the same energy differences.
The forms differ only when a hex crosses $\Theta$.
